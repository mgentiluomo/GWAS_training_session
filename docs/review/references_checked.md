## PCA deep dives (18 November): extra checks and items not used

- **Privé et al. 2020**, used again in steps 3.4, 5.4 and 5.7. The abstract (PubMed PMID 32415959, read again on 18 November 2026) says: pitfalls include LD structure captured instead of population structure, projected PCs that suffer from shrinkage bias, sample outliers and uneven population sizes; PC19 to PC40 of the UK Biobank capture complex LD structure; for a projection onto 1000 Genomes the shrinkage bias becomes large for PC5 and beyond; unbiased projections are obtained with the R package bigsnpr.
- **Patterson et al. 2006**, phase change. The full text was read in the JATS XML of Europe PMC (PMC1713260, open access), where the formulas are readable. Sentence used: "For two subpopulations of equal sample size, the phase change threshold is reached when 1/FST is equal to the square root of the data size D", with D the number of samples times the number of SNPs. So the threshold is Fst = 1/sqrt(n x m). The paper gives an example: with nm = 2^20 the threshold is Fst = 2^-10. The statement is asymptotic (large n and m). The course uses it in the Fst example: the mark on the Fst slider is 1/sqrt(people x variants). In the example, "Fst" is the Fst between the two simulated populations (each population drifts by twice that value, checked on simulated frequencies).
- **A published reply to Elhaik 2022 was not found.** I searched PubMed (title and "comment") and Crossref (author Shriner, with the topic of the article) and found no reply that I could verify. The course therefore cites none and names none. The text of the debate says that no reply is cited because none could be verified, and it asks students to ask their instructor. Crossref lists a later article by Mohseni and Elhaik in eLife 2024 (10.7554/elife.94685) with titles that start with "RETRACTED". It is not cited in the course.
- **Direction of the colour model effect (corrected).** The brief of 18 November was wrong, as the author found. In the article and in my implementation, Black moves towards the colour with fewer people in the PC1-PC2 plane. Checked in numbers: with 50 people of each colour, the distance from Black to a colour is 1.00 in three dimensions and 0.82 in the plane, and the first two PCs hold 88.9% of the variance (article: 0.82 and 88%). With 25 red and 50 green and 50 blue people, Black is at 0.71 from Red and 0.86 from Green and Blue in the plane. With 100 red people, Black is at 0.86 from Red and 0.79 from Green and Blue. Over all three PCs the distances stay at 1.00 in every case. Differences from the method of the article: (a) my sample sizes are 25, 50 and 100 with 50 black people, the article uses other sizes in its figures; (b) I show the mean position of each colour, not every person (the noise of 0.01 makes each colour a tiny cloud); (c) I did not reproduce the Cyan (admixed) cases or the human data sets of the article; (d) my PCA is prcomp with centring and no scaling, the article uses Matlab pca (singular value decomposition, with centring), which gives the same axes up to sign.
